# Strategy guide

Read `../../shared/method.md` first and follow it throughout.

## Purpose

Help an owner who has a decision to make but does not know which analysis they need. Choose the analyses that fit, run them in a useful order, and bring the results together. Do not run everything by default: most decisions need two or three analyses, not five.

## Step 1: Understand

Frame the question (method, section 2). Then ask, only if not already clear:

- What prompted this now?
- What has the owner already decided or ruled out?
- What do they already know about competitors and customers?

Keep it to one short round of questions. If a search tool is available, look up the business and its market briefly before choosing analyses, and tell the owner what you found.

## Step 2: Choose

Pick the analyses that fit the decision. Use this as a guide, not a rule:

| The decision is about | Usually needs |
|---|---|
| Entering a new market or area | PESTEL, Five Forces, growth options |
| Responding to a competitor or falling sales | Five Forces, Value chain, SWOT |
| Fixing margins, capacity or delivery problems | Value chain, then SWOT if choices follow |
| Launching a new product or service | Five Forces, Value chain, growth options |
| How to grow, with no fixed idea | Value chain, Five Forces, SWOT, growth options |
| A regulation or outside change | PESTEL, then SWOT |
| A general review of where the business stands | PESTEL, Five Forces, Value chain, SWOT |

Leave out an analysis when its answer would not change the decision.

**Choose at most two analyses for one reply.** Pick the two that bear most on the decision. More than two buries the answer. Offer the others in one line at the end as optional follow-ups.

Tell the owner the plan in a few lines: which analyses, in what order, and what each will add. Ask them to confirm or change it before starting. They can add or remove any analysis.

## Step 3: Run in order

The order matters, because each analysis feeds the next:

1. **PESTEL**: the wider world.
2. **Five Forces**: the industry and competitors.
3. **Value chain**: inside the business.
4. **SWOT**: where the outside and inside meet.
5. **Growth options**: choosing what to do.

Run each chosen analysis by following its own skill in this plugin (`../pestel-analysis/SKILL.md`, `../five-forces-analysis/SKILL.md`, `../value-chain-analysis/SKILL.md`, `../swot-analysis/SKILL.md`, `../growth-options-analysis/SKILL.md`) for its steps and checklist, but use the compact form below, not the full single-analysis shape.

**Compact form for each analysis inside a guided run:** a plain-English heading that says what the analysis looks at, the framework table with source tags, and at most three lines on what matters. Include only the rows that bear on the decision, and say in a few words what each row label means. No bottom line, no assumptions list, no next steps, no findings table and no limits per analysis: those appear once, in the summary. Never repeat a point already made in an earlier analysis; refer to it instead.

If the owner is present, pause after each analysis: give the two or three points that matter and ask whether anything is wrong or missing. Their corrections are the most valuable evidence available. If they ask to run straight through, or cannot reply, do so.

## Step 4: Bring it together

Shape of a guided reply, in this order:

1. **Bottom line.** Three to five lines answering the decision, with confidence. This comes first, before any analysis.
2. **Assumptions**, once.
3. **Recommended actions.** One table: Action, Why, Main risk, Impact (high, medium, low), Confidence, First step. Order by what to do first.
4. **How I approached it.** One line naming the two analyses used.
5. **The analyses**, in compact form.
6. **Where the analyses agree and disagree.** A few lines. State contradictions plainly, with a view on which evidence is stronger.
7. **One findings table** for the whole run (method, section 7), at most six rows, only if it adds something the bottom line and actions table do not already say.
8. **What to find out next**, and limits.

Aim for under 1,200 words in total.

The bottom line must include the result of testing the proposition (method, section 2): whether the owner's own figures add up. The actions table should include, where it fits, what to negotiate or find out before deciding, how to use the time before any deadline, and a clear rule for going ahead or walking away.

Check the summary before showing it:

- Does every statement in the bottom line and actions trace back to a finding in one of the analyses? Remove anything that appears only in the summary.
- Has any finding become more certain in the summary than it was in its analysis? Restore the original confidence.
- Are the recommended actions things this owner could start, given the constraints they stated?
- Is anything said twice? Cut the repeat.

## Limits

This guide selects and sequences analyses and summarises them. It does not replace judgement about the business, its people or its risks. Close with the line from the method, section 8.
