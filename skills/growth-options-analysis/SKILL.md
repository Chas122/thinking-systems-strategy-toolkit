# Growth options analysis (Ansoff Matrix)

Read `../../shared/method.md` first and follow it throughout.

## Purpose

Lay out the realistic ways this business could grow and compare them. The Ansoff Matrix sorts options by whether the products and the customers are existing or new:

| | Existing products | New products |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing markets** | Market penetration | Product development |
| **New markets** | Market development | Diversification |

Risk generally rises as you move away from what the business already knows, and is highest when both are new.

## Place in a sequence

Run last. It is a choice tool and needs evidence to choose with: SWOT implications, Five Forces findings for the market side, Value chain findings for what the business can actually deliver. If none exist, run it now and note in one line under Limits what would strengthen it.

## How to run it

1. Frame the question (method, section 2). Establish what growth means to this owner (revenue, profit, resilience, freedom from one big customer) and how much money and time they can commit.
2. Generate concrete options in each of the four boxes. Use the owner's own ideas first, then add others suggested by earlier findings. An option is a specific move, for example "offer the monthly retainer to existing one-off clients", not a category.
3. For each option, assess: what it would take (money, people, time), how well it fits what the business is good at, the main risk, and what evidence supports demand.
4. Include doing nothing, or consolidating, as an option where growth itself is in question.
5. Compare the options against what the owner said growth means and what they can commit.
6. Recommend an order of attention, not a single answer: what to pursue, what to test cheaply first, what to park. Say what would change the recommendation.
7. Turn the recommendation into a plan that covers the owner's whole time horizon in stages, says how the stated budget is split across the stages and how much is held in reserve, and gives a stop rule for each test. Mark every cost and return as [Assumed] with its sum unless the owner supplied it.

## Checklist for this analysis

- Is each option a specific move this business could make, not a restatement of the box title?
- Is each option placed in the right box? Check whether the product and the customer group are truly new to this business.
- Does each option name its main risk and its cost in terms the owner gave?
- Is evidence of demand tagged, and are [Assumed] demand claims turned into cheap tests?
- Does the comparison use SWOT, Five Forces and Value chain findings where they exist, by reference?
- Is diversification treated with proper caution, and not presented as the exciting choice?
- Does the recommendation follow from the evidence shown, and state what would change it?
- Does the plan cover the whole horizon and account for the whole budget, including what is held back?
- Does each recommended move name its downside, including any price change?

## Output

Follow the shape in the method, section 6. The analysis part contains:

1. The four-box table with the concrete options in each.
2. A comparison table: Option, What it takes, Fit with strengths, Main risk, Evidence of demand, Source.
3. A staged plan: what to do in each period of the horizon, the budget for each stage, the reserve, the stop rules, and what would change the view.
