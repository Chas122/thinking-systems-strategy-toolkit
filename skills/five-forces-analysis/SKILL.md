# Five Forces analysis

Read `../../shared/method.md` first and follow it throughout.

## Purpose

Work out how hard it is to make a living in this industry, and where the pressure comes from. The five forces are: rivalry among existing competitors, the threat of new entrants, the threat of substitutes, the bargaining power of suppliers, and the bargaining power of customers.

## Place in a sequence

Run after PESTEL if there is one, and before SWOT. Its findings feed the Opportunities and Threats of a SWOT and the market side of a growth options analysis.

## How to run it

1. Frame the question (method, section 2).
2. **Define the industry narrowly.** Use the market the business actually competes in, by product and by geography. "Cafes within walking distance of the station" is better than "hospitality".
3. Name the real competitors. Ask the owner who they lose business to. Search for others when a search tool is available.
4. For each force, rate the pressure as high, medium or low, with the evidence.
5. **Shift perspective.** Describe the business as its strongest competitor would see it: what would that competitor attack, and what would they leave alone?
6. Say which one or two forces shape profit most, and whether they are getting stronger or weaker.

## Checklist for this analysis

- Are all five forces covered? Substitutes are the one most often missed: include different ways the customer could meet the same need, including doing nothing or doing it themselves.
- Is the industry defined narrowly enough that the competitors named are ones the customer would really choose between?
- Are competitors named, with a source, and not described as "various competitors"?
- Is each rating backed by evidence, not by impression?
- Does the analysis use PESTEL findings where they exist, by row number?
- Is the competitor's view included, and is it uncomfortable enough to be useful?

## Output

Follow the shape in the method, section 6. The analysis part contains:

1. One table: Force, Pressure (high, medium, low), Evidence, Direction of change, Source.
2. A short paragraph: the view from the strongest competitor.
3. A short paragraph on the one or two forces that matter most.
