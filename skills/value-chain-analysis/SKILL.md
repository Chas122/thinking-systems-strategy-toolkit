# Value chain analysis

Read `../../shared/method.md` first and follow it throughout.

## Purpose

Look inside the business. Follow the work from first contact with a customer to getting paid and keeping them, and find which steps customers value, which steps cost more than they return, and which the business does better or worse than competitors.

## Place in a sequence

Run before SWOT. Its findings feed the Strengths and Weaknesses of a SWOT and the capability side of a growth options analysis. It does not depend on PESTEL or Five Forces, though competitor findings help with step 5.

## How to run it

1. Frame the question (method, section 2).
2. **Adapt the chain to the business.** The textbook version was drawn for manufacturers. For a service or small business, use the steps that actually happen, for example: finding customers, selling, delivering the work, getting paid, looking after customers afterwards. Add the support activities that matter: people, systems and tools, buying, management and finance.
3. Ask the owner to describe each step as it really works. This lens depends on [Owner] evidence more than any other. If the owner has not described a step, ask; do not guess.
4. For each step, note what the customer values in it, roughly what it costs in money or time, and how well it works.
5. Where possible, compare with competitors: is this step better, the same, or worse than theirs?
6. Look at the links between steps. Problems often sit in the handover, not in the step.
7. Identify the one or two steps that set the business apart, and the one or two that hold it back.

## Checklist for this analysis

- Does the chain describe this business's real steps, not the textbook categories forced to fit?
- Is each strength something customers notice or pay for, not just something the owner is proud of?
- Is each weakness described by its effect (lost sales, delay, cost, risk), not just named?
- Is anything about the internal workings of the business tagged [Assumed]? If so, turn it into a question for the owner.
- Are the handovers between steps covered?
- Is everything here internal? Market conditions and competitor moves belong in other analyses.

## Output

Follow the shape in the method, section 6. The analysis part contains:

1. One table: Step, What the customer values, Cost or effort, How well it works, Compared with competitors, Source.
2. A short paragraph on what sets the business apart and what holds it back.
