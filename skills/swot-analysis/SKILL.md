# SWOT analysis

Read `../../shared/method.md` first and follow it throughout.

## Purpose

Bring the internal picture (Strengths, Weaknesses) and the external picture (Opportunities, Threats) together, then work out what the combinations mean for the decision. A SWOT is a meeting point for evidence, not a starting point: its quality depends on what feeds it.

## Place in a sequence

Run after the external and internal analyses when they exist:

- Opportunities and Threats come from PESTEL and Five Forces findings.
- Strengths and Weaknesses come from Value chain findings.

If none exist, run the SWOT now and note in one line under Limits that a competitor analysis and an internal review would strengthen it. Its findings feed a growth options analysis.

## How to run it

1. Frame the question (method, section 2).
2. Collect earlier findings tables from the conversation and sort them into the four boxes using their "Bears on" column, keeping references, source tags and confidence.
3. Fill gaps by asking the owner and, for external factors, by searching.
4. Apply the tests:
   - **Internal or external?** Strengths and Weaknesses are things the business has, does or chooses: its people, the owner's health and hours, its prices, a loan it decides to take. Opportunities and Threats would exist even if this business did not.
   - **Compared with whom?** A strength is only a strength relative to competitors or to what customers expect.
   - **Opportunity or wish?** An opportunity is a change in the outside world the business could use, not an action the owner would like to take.
5. Keep each box to the two or three items that matter most for the decision, each in 15 words or fewer. The word budget in the method, section 6, covers both tables together.
6. **Combine.** This is the step that makes a SWOT useful:
   - Strengths with Opportunities: where to push.
   - Strengths with Threats: what to defend with.
   - Weaknesses with Opportunities: what must be fixed first.
   - Weaknesses with Threats: where the business is exposed.
7. State what the combinations suggest for the decision in hand. This is the answer at the top of the reply.

## Checklist for this analysis

- Is every Strength and Weakness internal, and every Opportunity and Threat external? Move any that are in the wrong box.
- Is each Strength real relative to competitors, with evidence, not a claim every rival would also make ("good service", "experienced team")?
- Is each Opportunity distinct from the Strengths, and a change in the outside world, not a plan?
- Does each Weakness say what it costs the business?
- Are there contradictions, such as the same thing listed as a strength and a weakness? Resolve or explain them.
- Has the combining step been done, and does it lead to something the owner could act on?
- Do items taken from earlier analyses keep their original confidence?

## Output

Follow the shape in the method, section 6. The analysis part contains:

1. The SWOT as a two-by-two table, each item with its source tag and, where it came from an earlier analysis, the reference.
2. A second table for the four combinations, with one implication each. Label its rows in plain words only: "Where to push", "What to defend with", "What to fix first", "Where you are exposed". Do not print the pairings ("Strengths with Threats") to the owner.
3. No closing paragraph: what this means for the decision is the answer at the top. Do not say it twice.
